## Agenda+ Process

Alan and I have been asked how we determine what issues should be discussed at any given upcoming CSSWG Meeting. I figured I'd write up a quick README on the topic to keep everything as transparent as possible. 

### Weekly Conference Calls
At the end of each conference call, we copy/paste the issues that did not get discussed into an email and send that email to the other co-chair. 
That list is the start of the next week's agenda.
Assuming it looks like the group will be able to get through all of those remaining issues, we go to the [Agenda+ query in github](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues?q=is%3Aissue%20state%3Aopen%20label%3AAgenda%2B). Once there, we look at the oldest issues first and read through their most recent comments. If it looks like something we can close during one of our calls, we add it to the proposed agenda.

Since we typically go back to the oldest issues first, some of the newest and (potentially) most pressing issues may not make the agenda. To account for this, we always send out the proposed agenda two days before the call and we ask for feedback. It is not uncommon for someone in the group to request we add something.

We keep track of the scribe order by modifying the list [here](https://wiki.csswg.org/planning/scribing/) after each meeting as well.

### Face-to-Face meetings and TPAC
For Face-to-Face meetings we look at the group of issues that have been tagged with F2F or TPAC and try to determine groupings. We ask for everyone to RSVP for the F2F to verify who will be there in person and who will need to attend remotely. This can help us determine (based on timezones) when would be the best time to have certain groups of discussions. Then we create a project in Github and add the issues to a kanban-style board.

### Ongoing Triage
One issue we face currently is that there are too many issues that have been tagged for Agenda+.
We have recently begun to triage issues by looking at them in a [kanban board on github](https://github.com/orgs/w3c/projects/253/views/1?filterQuery=). 
We haven't had a chance to really put it to the test because we created it just before TPAC 2026. However, it is the hope we can identify issues that need to be discussed urgently, issues that are ready but not urgent, issues that have proposed resolutions, and issues that are just bikeshedding. By categorizing them, we hope to be able to clear the list more quickly than we currently do.

### Feedback
We would also love to hear feedback on this process. We do not want to accidentally quiet the concern of someone simply for the sake of clearing out our backlog of issues. But we also recognize that some of the issues are likely no longer relevant, not as important as others, or already resolved but mis-marked.
